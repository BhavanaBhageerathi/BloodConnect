BLOOD DONATION BASICS

What is blood donation?
Blood donation is the voluntary collection of blood at an authorized blood centre or approved donation camp for transfusion or preparation of blood components.

Why is blood needed?
Blood and components may be needed for severe blood loss, surgery, childbirth-related complications, some cancers, severe anemia, and certain blood disorders. The treating clinician decides which component is needed.

Whole blood and components
Whole blood may be processed into red blood cells, platelets, and plasma. Different components have different clinical uses and storage requirements.

General criteria commonly used in India
- Age: commonly 18–65 years; first-time donors generally should not be over 60.
- Weight: at least 45 kg for a 350 mL collection; larger collection volumes require higher weight criteria.
- Health: the donor should be well and medically suitable on donation day.
- Whole-blood interval: commonly 90 days for males and 120 days for females under current Indian guidance.
These are general reference criteria, not an eligibility decision. Criteria may vary by donation type and current rules. The blood-centre medical officer decides.

Screening
Staff may ask confidential questions about health, medicines, travel, procedures, and relevant risk factors. They may check temperature, pulse, blood pressure, weight, and haemoglobin.

Typical process
1. Registration and identity check.
2. Confidential questionnaire and screening.
3. Explanation and informed consent.
4. Cleaning of the collection site and use of sterile single-use equipment.
5. Blood collection by trained staff.
6. Rest and refreshments with aftercare instructions.

Before donation
Eat a normal meal, drink water, sleep adequately, bring required identification, and answer screening questions honestly. Inform staff about medicines, recent illness, travel, procedures, or concerns.

After donation
Follow staff instructions, rest as advised, drink fluids over the next day or two, eat a balanced meal, and avoid strenuous activity/heavy lifting for several hours or as instructed. If dizzy, sit or lie down and alert staff. Seek medical advice for persistent, severe, or concerning symptoms.

Common temporary effects
Bruising, mild soreness, brief light-headedness, or fatigue can occur. Tell staff if you feel unwell.

Safety
Authorized centres use sterile single-use collection equipment and follow blood-safety procedures. Donated blood is screened and processed according to applicable requirements.

Blood groups
ABO and Rh groups are important, but blood-group labels alone do not determine transfusion compatibility. Blood-bank testing and crossmatching are required. Never use chatbot advice to select a blood unit.

FAQ
Q: Does donation hurt?
A: A brief needle pinch may occur. Staff can explain the process and help with discomfort.

Q: Can I donate with a cold or fever?
A: Do not donate while ill. Tell the blood centre and follow its deferral advice.

Q: Can I donate while taking medication?
A: Eligibility depends on the medication, condition, dose, and reason. Tell screening staff; do not stop prescribed medication to donate.

Q: Can I donate during pregnancy?
A: Pregnancy is generally a reason to defer. Ask the blood centre about postpartum or breastfeeding timing.

Q: What about recent tattoo, piercing, surgery, transfusion, dental procedure, vaccination, or travel?
A: Deferral periods vary. Disclose details and let the blood centre decide.

Q: Can someone with diabetes, high blood pressure, or a chronic condition donate?
A: It depends on control, treatment, complications, and centre rules. The medical officer decides.

Q: Is donated blood tested?
A: Blood centres screen for specified transfusion-transmissible infections and perform required grouping and processing. Screening does not replace honest donor disclosure.

Q: Can the chatbot confirm eligibility?
A: No. It can explain general criteria, but screening staff make the final decision.
