DONOR ELIGIBILITY AND DEFERRAL

Educational only. Donor selection is performed by the blood centre under current national rules and approved procedures.

General criteria commonly used in India
- At least 18 years old.
- Maximum age generally 65; first-time donors generally should not be over 60.
- Minimum weight commonly 45 kg for a 350 mL collection; larger collection volumes require higher weight.
- In good health and feeling well on donation day.
- Screening measures such as haemoglobin, pulse, blood pressure, and temperature must meet the centre's requirements.
- Whole-blood intervals commonly used in Indian guidance are 90 days for males and 120 days for females.
Rules may change and differ by donation type. The blood centre decides.

Do not donate without assessment if you have fever, acute infection, feel unwell, unexplained symptoms, a condition affecting donation, pregnancy or recent childbirth, a recent surgery/transfusion/tattoo/piercing/dental procedure/vaccination/travel, medication use, possible blood-borne infection exposure, low haemoglobin, or a history of fainting or other concerns.

Medication
Do not stop or change prescribed medicines to become eligible. Tell screening staff the medicine, dose, and reason.

Deferral
Deferral may be temporary or permanent depending on the condition, medication, exposure, procedure, and blood-centre policy. Ask the blood centre when you may be eligible again.

Confidentiality
Answer donor-screening questions honestly. If concerned about infection exposure, seek appropriate healthcare and testing. Do not rely on donation screening as a diagnostic test.

Chatbot rule
Never give a definitive personal eligibility decision. Explain general criteria and direct users to a licensed blood centre. For urgent symptoms, advise urgent medical care.
